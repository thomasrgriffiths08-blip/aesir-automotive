Straight off the bench

The Work

Every photo below comes straight from the workshop's own Instagram, @aesir.automotive — real jobs at Pond Farm, photographed as they happened. No stock photos, no staging. The site makes claims; this page is the proof.

The claims, with the proof
E60 M5 Touring — S85 V10 bottom end

The site says engines most garages won't open are rebuilt here. This is what that looks like: all the big-end shells out of BMW's F1-derived V10, laid out in firing order. The bronze showing through on the right-hand set is the bearing overlay running out — caught before it scored the crank. Neil's own caption: "close shave or what?"

As posted · @aesir.automotive · Mar 2025
Z3 M Coupé — S50B32 engine-out rebuild

Not a spark-plug service: the M Coupé in the air with its S50 straight-six out of the car in the foreground. Stripped, rebuilt and refitted at Pond Farm — "fired straight up, and ran sweet from the get go."

As posted · @aesir.automotive · Nov 2023
Machining, not just fitting parts

A straight-six block back from machining — deck skimmed flat, bores honed to a mirror. One frame, two claims proved: proper engine machining on the bench, and the red Ferrari F430 waiting on the ramp behind it.

As posted · @aesir.automotive · Nov 2023
Ferrari F430 — the diagnosis others couldn't land

The F430 Spider inside the workshop, doors up. It arrived with multiple misfires other garages couldn't trace — and left fixed. Proper diagnosis doesn't care about the badge.

As posted · @aesir.automotive · Nov 2023
Rolls-Royce Ghost — Spirit of Ecstasy repair

The gold-finished housing for the retracting mascot mechanism, off the car and on the bench next to the Ghost's grille. The same visit traced and sealed its oil leaks — BMW-group engineering at its grandest, handled at Pond Farm.

As posted · @aesir.automotive
A proper workshop, not an arch with a jack

Pond Farm from the inside: two-post lifts, a blast cabinet for cleaning castings, press, and a wall of Snap-on boxes. This is the floor the engines above were rebuilt on — the reason engine-out work happens in-house, not farmed out.
